Toast vs snackbar

Mostly no. Both words describe a short message that appears over the page, then goes away on its own. The difference is mainly which design tradition you learned the word from.

Where the names come from

Toast is the common word on the web and in a lot of component libraries, named after bread popping out of a toaster. Snackbar is the term Material Design uses, and libraries built on Material follow it.

Where people draw a line

Some teams use snackbar for a single message fixed to a screen edge, usually with one action, and toast for any floating message including ones that stack. That is a team convention, not a standard. If your design system already picked a word, use it everywhere, in code, docs and conversation.

What to actually decide

The name matters less than the behavior. Decide whether messages stack or replace each other, where they appear, how long they stay, and whether they can carry an action. Ding is a toast library that shows one message at a time and replaces it on the next call. The UX guide goes through those choices.
